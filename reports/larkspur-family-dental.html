Executive summary

The weakest areas are Security Awareness Training (11.1%) and Access Control (16.7%). There are 10 quick wins that can be completed in the next 30 days at low cost. Doing them lowers the risk index from 85.1 to 39.9, a reduction of 45.2 points. Completing the full plan brings the risk index down to 0.

Remediation plan

Now (0-30 days) risk index 85.1 → 39.9

#ActionEffortCostRisk ↓
1Restore testing
Run a test restore of one critical system every six months and record how long it takes.
Many businesses only discover their backups are broken in the middle of an emergency.		Low$−16
2Security training
Require short security training for every new hire and an annual refresher for all staff.
Most breaches start with a person being tricked. Trained staff spot and report attacks earlier.		Low$−16
3Multi-factor authentication
Turn on MFA for every user's email and remote access, starting with admin accounts.
Stolen passwords are the most common way attackers get in. MFA stops most of those attempts even when the password is known.		Low$−15
4Account offboarding
Create an offboarding checklist that disables all accounts on the employee's last day, and review active accounts quarterly.
Former employees' accounts are an open door. Nobody notices when they're misused.		Low$−15
5Device encryption
Turn on built-in full-disk encryption (BitLocker or FileVault) on every laptop and enforce it centrally.
A lost or stolen laptop without encryption is a data breach. With encryption, it's just a lost device.		Low$−15
6Emergency contacts
Keep a printed and offline list of emergency contacts: IT support, cyber insurer, legal counsel, and key vendors.
During an attack, email and shared drives may be unavailable exactly when the contacts are needed.		Low$−12
7Software updates
Turn on automatic updates for all computers and apply critical security patches within 14 days.
Attackers exploit known vulnerabilities within days of a patch being released. Unpatched systems are the easiest targets.		Low$−12
8Password management
Roll out a company password manager and require unique passwords for every system.
Reused or shared passwords mean one breach anywhere unlocks accounts everywhere.		Low$−12
9Phishing simulations
Run quarterly phishing simulations and track the click and report rates over time.
Practice makes phishing recognition a habit, and the results show where extra training is needed.		Low$−12
10Incident reporting channel
Set up one clear reporting channel, such as a report-phishing button or a security email address, and announce it to all staff.
Fast reporting shortens how long an attacker has before they're stopped.		Low$−9

11Sensitive data access
Restrict sensitive folders and systems to the roles that need them, and review who has access twice a year.
Limiting access reduces how much data one compromised account or careless mistake can expose.		Medium$−20
12Admin rights
Remove local admin rights from everyday accounts and give IT separate admin logins.
When a user with admin rights clicks a malicious link, the attacker gets full control of the computer.		Medium$−16
13Incident response plan
Write a two-page incident response plan covering who decides, who to call, and first steps, then walk through it once a year.
Decisions made calmly in advance are faster and better than decisions made mid-crisis.		Medium$−16
14Backup frequency
Set up automatic daily backups of all critical systems and shared files.
Without recent backups, ransomware or a failed drive can mean permanent loss of records and days of downtime.		Medium$$−15
15Offline / immutable backups
Keep at least one backup copy offline or in immutable cloud storage that ordinary accounts can't delete.
Ransomware deliberately looks for connected backups and encrypts them too. A separate copy is what makes recovery possible.		Medium$$−15
16Endpoint protection
Deploy managed endpoint protection on every device, with alerts sent to IT or your service provider.
Endpoint protection catches and blocks most common malware before it spreads.		Medium$$−12
17Asset inventory
Build a simple inventory of every device and key application, with its owner, and review it quarterly.
You can't patch, back up, or protect what you don't know you have.		Medium$−12
18End-of-life software
Identify all end-of-life systems and budget to replace or isolate them within the next year.
Unsupported software no longer receives security fixes, so every new vulnerability stays open permanently.		High$$$−12

Method

Each control is scored 0-3 for maturity and mapped to NIST CSF 2.0. Likelihood (1-5) comes from maturity; impact (1-5) is set per control and raised by 1 for data-related controls when sensitive data is held. Risk = likelihood × impact (Low 1-5, Medium 6-11, High 12-19, Critical 20-25). The risk index scales total risk from 0 (every control strong) to 100 (every control missing). Actions are ranked by risk reduction ÷ effort.
